## Competitive Position

AMD has credible share gains in x86 CPUs: latest full report cites Mercury/Tom's Hardware data showing Q4 2025 server CPU unit share 28.8% and server CPU revenue share 41.3%. For AI accelerators, no reliable public open-source share number was used; Nvidia remains the dominant incumbent, while AMD is trying to become the preferred second source and custom hyperscaler partner.

## Moat

- EPYC performance, power efficiency and server CPU share gains.
- OpenAI and Meta 6GW partnerships validating Instinct/MI450 at hyperscaler scale.
- Broader platform stack across GPUs, CPUs, Pensando networking, ROCm and Helios.
- Fabless access to leading-edge TSMC nodes.
- Xilinx/adaptive computing portfolio gives exposure beyond PCs and gaming.

## Key Risks

- Valuation is stretched after a large YTD/12-month rally.
- Q1 FY2026 earnings on 2026-05-05 are a near-term event risk.
- MI450/OpenAI/Meta ramp begins mainly in 2H 2026, so investors are paying ahead of proof.
- Nvidia software/system moat remains the biggest competitive barrier.
- Supply constraints in TSMC capacity, HBM, packaging, substrates and rack deployment can limit growth.
- Export control risk can hit China-facing AI GPU shipments, inventory and margins.
- Intel can regain some CPU share if product/process execution improves.
- Recent insider activity is selling-heavy rather than buying-heavy.
- SET DR execution still requires live spread/indicative-value checks.

## Valuation Notes
