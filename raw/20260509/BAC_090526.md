### Why BAC Is Cheaper Than JPM

BAC is cheaper because the market pays less for a bank with lower ROTCE and more sensitivity to NII catch-up and consumer credit. JPM's latest vault report used a reference P/TBV around 2.9x and ROTCE 23%, while BAC at the current price is about 1.78x P/TBV and Q1 ROTCE 16.0%; these are not the same quality/valuation setup <cite>[[JPM_060526]]</cite> <cite>[Bank of America 1Q26 8-K press release](https://investor.bankofamerica.com/regulatory-and-other-filings/select-sec-filings/content/0000070858-26-000222/bac03312026ex991.htm)</cite>.

Bank math sanity check:

```text
Current P/TBV calculation = $51.31 / $28.84 = 1.78x
Implied P/E from P/TBV and ROTCE = 1.78 / 16.0% = 11.1x
```

This roughly matches the forward P/E range. So BAC is not "mysteriously cheap"; it is priced like a bank that can earn mid-teens ROTCE but does not deserve JPM's premium unless its revenue growth, deposits, credit and capital return keep proving out.

## Long-Term Outlook

BAC can compound if three things happen together:

1. **NII recovery continues:** Q1 NII was $15.7B GAAP and up 9% YoY <cite>[Bank of America 1Q26 8-K press release](https://investor.bankofamerica.com/regulatory-and-other-filings/select-sec-filings/content/0000070858-26-000222/bac03312026ex991.htm)</cite>.

3. **Buybacks are accretive:** Q1 share repurchases were about $7.2B, while shares outstanding declined from 7.560B in Q1 2025 to 7.130B at Q1 2026 period-end <cite>[Bank of America 1Q26 8-K press release](https://investor.bankofamerica.com/regulatory-and-other-filings/select-sec-filings/content/0000070858-26-000222/bac03312026ex991.htm)</cite>.

The key difference versus JPM is that BAC has more rerating potential if ROTCE improves from mid-teens while valuation stays near 1.7x-1.8x tangible book. The key danger is that a bank can look optically cheap right before EPS falls. For a moderate-risk Thai investor, BAC should be **financials satellite/core-candidate starter**, not a maximum-size bet.

## Verdict

Bank of America is a better **price discipline buy** than a chase. Q1 2026 showed the right things: NII growth, EPS growth, capital return, healthy deposits, broad segment contribution and improving efficiency. Valuation is not stretched: P/TBV around 1.78x and forward P/E around 11.3x can work if ROTCE stays mid-teens.

But BAC is still a bank. Credit, deposits, rates, capital rules, litigation and capital-markets normalization can turn "cheap P/E" into a value trap. The correct move for Mangkorn is **starter sizing only**:

- **Buy 10%-15% starter at $49-$51** or BAC03 fair equivalent.
- **Add at $45-$48** only if credit/NII/CET1/ROTCE thesis remains intact.
- **Do not chase above $55**.
- **Trim $65-$71** if re-rating arrives before earnings/tangible book catch up.
- **Stop/reassess around $42** or on thesis break.
